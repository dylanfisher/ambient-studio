Model test

A model test checks how well the local model follows your briefs, without making any audio. For each brief you tick, it asks the model for the number of candidates you set, checks whether each answer follows the format and the brief's rules, gives a failed answer one chance to fix it, and reports how often that worked and how long each request took. Run one after changing a brief's rules, or when sessions give many rejected candidates. To get something to listen to, start a session from a brief instead. With the revision test ticked it also renders each answer's excerpt, and sends every one that misses one of the brief's feature ranges back to the model twice: once with its measurements and once without, to find out whether hearing the measurements helps. The taste model test, further down, checks something else: how well the studio has learned your own A/B choices, and the optimizer test checks whether a search scored by that model finds better candidates than plain nudges. Each result opens with what it found in words; Show the numbers gives its rates, times and tables.

Fit your taste model

Your taste model learns from your A/B choices and thumbs. First analyze the sound of every rated excerpt and candidate excerpt that isn't analyzed yet, then fit the model for every brief. Fit again after rating more: the model is out of date as soon as a new rating lands. A brief's page then says what you like, and its next candidates are listed by the model's score. Both run on this computer's processor, so they never hold the model the website shares; the first analysis downloads the sound-analysis model, about 0.8 GB.

Test your taste model

For each brief, this learns your taste from every session but one, then checks how often it picks the side you chose in the session it left out, one session at a time. It compares that with three simple guesses: always the left side, always the louder side, and the same model without the sound analysis. About-the-same choices aren't counted. It needs 40 A/B choices over at least 3 sessions per brief, and it runs on this computer's processor, so it never holds the model the website shares.

Test the optimizer

For each brief you tick, this runs 3 searches, each scored by your taste model, and after each one the same number of plain medium nudges of the same start as a control. It then compares the two bests of each search, by the model and by your ears. The model's comparison counts only once the taste model test above finds the model picks your choices more often than chance. For your ears it queues 12 blind pairs per brief, 4 per search, in Quick A/B: you choose without knowing which side is which, and the result needs 12 decided pairs. A brief needs a fitted taste model, and starts from the candidates you name or, with the field empty, its best-ranked ones. It runs on this computer's processor and never uses the shared model, but renders every point, so it takes from minutes to hours; the confirm says how long.

    What this means
